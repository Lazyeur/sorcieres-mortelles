![The three members of Starwolf talking in a turquoise room with a checkered floor](/img/starwolf-groupe.jpg "Starwolf. © Starwolf")

Q: Let’s start with the title: your music gets a little closer to Brazil with every project, and this album is literally called *Copacabana*. Where does this connection with Brazilian music come from?

R: During the pandemic I spent a lot of time going down musical rabbit holes. I’d spend hours listening to genres that I previously hadn’t given much attention to. I think 1970s Brazilian jazz/funk/disco music had the biggest impact on me. Artists like Tim Maia, Marcos Valle, Helio Matheus, Robson Jorge/Lincoln Olivetti have had a big influence on our sound.

![A rainbow macaw flying through space among planets, trailing a beam of rainbow light](/img/starwolf-copacabana.jpg "Cover of *Copacabana*. © Starwolf")

Q: Six of the eight tracks on *Copacabana* came out as singles over about a year and a half. Did you have the album in mind from the start, or did it take shape single by single? And what does releasing music so regularly change for an independent band?

R: We released each single in the order that they were completed. I knew that it was going to culminate into a full album, but I didn’t always know what direction it was going to take. I actually enjoy releasing most of our songs as singles because I think it gives each song a chance to stand out on its own. People don’t always have the attention spans to make it through a full album, so inevitably some songs end up getting skipped over.

![The word “Starwolf” glowing in yellow above a pair of blue legs running between giant psychedelic mushrooms, with “Astro Lobo” underneath](/img/starwolf-astro-lobo.jpg "*Astro Lobo*. © Starwolf")

Q: If I understand correctly, *Astro Lobo* was the last album you recorded together in the same room, and *Tropical Disco* was largely built in Max’s basement studio. Were you able to make *Copacabana* together this time? How did that change the way the album came together?

R: *Copacabana* was made in a similar way as *Tropical Disco*, in that I wrote the songs alone in my home studio. A big part of that is because I probably have the most free time haha. However, Tim and Chris were still able to contribute. I should probably also give a shoutout to our friend Tim Lefebvre who played bass on most of the songs, and our friend Deanna Sorenson who added additional vocals to most of the album.

![Golden disco balls floating in a starry sky](/img/starwolf-tropical-disco.jpg "Cover of *Tropical Disco*. © Starwolf")

Q: Max, I read that you spent 80 hours on a single flute solo for “Moon Castle.” Was there a track on *Copacabana* that needed the same kind of obsession?

R: I think on *Copacabana* the song that took the most time was “Back It Up.” I struggled with finding the best chorus for that song. I ended up recording a couple dozen different versions until I finally landed on the chorus that’s there now. But, overall I tried to not overthink as much on this album.

Q: Do you dance a lot to your own songs in the studio?

R: Haha yes! But, only when I’m getting excited about something new that I wrote.

Q: Do you get to make as much music as you’d like?

R: Unfortunately, no. It usually takes me at least a few hundred hours to complete each song, so completing a whole album takes longer than I’d like.

![A Starwolf musician in sunglasses singing and playing bass on stage, under blue lights](/img/starwolf-scene.jpg "Starwolf on stage. © Starwolf")

Q: How would you describe “yacht rock” in your own words?

R: Easy listening meets jazz meets funk.

Q: *St. Louis Magazine* called Starwolf “a band out of time.” How do you bring that into the way you present yourselves, on social media for example? I’m thinking of that photo with the aging filter that people thought was real.

R: Haha that photo was just a joke that got out of hand, but ended up helping our music reach a larger audience. I do have a soft spot in my heart for 1970s music. I think it was the golden age for music, which definitely has a huge influence on us and our sound. Recordings from the 70s, and photography from the 70s have always resonated with me, so we try to pay tribute to that time period.

Q: If you could only wear clothes of one color from now on, which color would it be?

R: Definitely green!

Q: The motto of Sorcières Mortelles is “All of Us Witches, All of Us Artists.” Do you think of your music as a kind of magic?

R: I do! I think music has the power to change your mood, feelings, etc. For me, making music is the best escape from reality.

Q: And the last question we ask everyone: which artist would you recommend to our readers?

R: Hm… I’d say Helio Matheus. Also, Martin Denny.
