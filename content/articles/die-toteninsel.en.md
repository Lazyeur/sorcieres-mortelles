**Do you know this island?**

Look at this painting. Sink into its cypresses. Follow that pale figure drawing near.

![A boat carries a figure draped in white towards a rocky island covered in dark cypresses, under a stormy sky](/img/ile-des-morts.jpg "*Isle of the Dead* (third version, 1883), Arnold Böcklin. Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin.")

The peaceful sea, the gentle sunset, the imposing monoliths carved with ancient tombs. A deep calm emanates from this painting; we seem to know this place. Without any effort, we can imagine ourselves wandering this island, feeling the pleasant sea breeze, hearing the branches of the trees brushing against one another.

And yet, this islet seems to hold a secret. The curious entrances, but above all that immense dark mass of the cypresses, alluring, hypnotic.

The work speaks for itself, its theme is clear. This island represents death.

I won’t introduce its painter or its history here: many articles and videos already do so very well (you’ll find links about the painting at the end of the article).

We are used to being exposed to art about death and the afterlife. But these works always present it as something sad, frightening, terrifying, haunting.

Some fear it. The Last Judgement, the end of the mind, wandering in limbo.
Others are ready to welcome it: paradise, reincarnation, or once again the end of the mind.

Personally, I find some of these beliefs terrifying. The idea of an infinite consciousness, living on in a separate plane, or endlessly returning to Earth. An infinite existence with no rest at all. I find that idea frightening and, above all, disheartening.

Death always stirs strong emotions.

Yet here, it is quite the opposite: death is reassuring.

The painting offers us a new angle on this fate that will touch us all. No danger, no judgement. Just the island, and its caves ready to welcome you.
Why not accept death? Why flee an event that every form of life must go through?

Is the white figure accompanied by Charon a woman in mourning bringing her late husband to the island? Or the dead one himself, gazing at his new home?

This figure has resonated, and still resonates, throughout humanity. This painting has been an obsession ever since it was created. A great many artists have taken on the exercise of making their own version, because the work fishes out a nostalgia for an unknown moment. If this article begins by asking whether you know this place, it is precisely because it awakens a ghostly memory in each and every one of us.

You will find references to this work in paintings, films, comics, manga, music, writing… All of them wanted to pay tribute to this creation.

Once you know its codes, its tall cypresses, its imposing cliffs and the solitude of this floating piece of rock, you will see it everywhere.

The painter was aware of what he had achieved. On one of the many versions he painted, above one of the entrances in the rock, he carved his initials, as if choosing his own tomb.
As if he knew that this painting would be what kept him alive, in the minds of each and every one of us.

![Detail of the painting: above an entrance cut into the rock, the letters A and B](/img/ile-des-morts-initiales.jpg "Detail of *Isle of the Dead* (1883): the painter’s initials.")
