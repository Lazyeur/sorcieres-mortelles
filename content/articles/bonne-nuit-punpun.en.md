Hi everyone!

Today I’d like to talk about Inio Asano, a small, barely known manga artist (not true, obviously) whom I particularly like, notably because he wrote and drew the wonderful work that is *Goodnight Punpun*, which we’re going to dwell on in this article.

First of all, there will be spoilers. I’m not going to try to tell everything either, but I’ll inevitably spoil things to talk about what I love in *Goodnight Punpun*. I’ll be talking about how I feel about the work, so you’re free to disagree with me (you should know that *Goodnight Punpun* is my favourite manga).

**Trigger warning**: this manga deals with very heavy themes, such as dysfunctional families, loneliness and depression.

## The synopsis

Punpun is a young man (who looks like a sort of bird in the manga). We follow his life from childhood to the end of adolescence, between his first love, his family situation, his friendships… So we step into Punpun’s life, which is going to be eventful!

![Yellow cover: the outline of a bird drawn in thick ochre strokes, with the title Bonne nuit Punpun written vertically](/img/punpun-tome-1.jpg "Cover of volume 1 of *Goodnight Punpun*, Inio Asano (French edition, Kana).")

In the end, the synopsis is pretty short, since the heart of the story really lies in Punpun’s life. So I’ll have to spoil things to get straight to the juicy part and to what I’d like to talk about.

## Punpun is everyone

In fact, *Goodnight Punpun* isn’t just a manga about adolescence: on top of that, you can identify with the main character. If Punpun is drawn as a bird, it’s no accident: the author never shows the protagonist’s real face, so that we can imagine ourselves in his place. Punpun is everyone, and everyone can be Punpun. On top of that, we never see Punpun speak in a speech bubble; we sometimes get his thoughts, but that’s all. And yet Punpun is one of the most developed characters in the work, if not the most developed, both emotionally and in his relationships.

Especially since, at the start, you think he’s living something fairly ordinary. Punpun lives with his parents (well, with his mother, who drinks a little, and with his uncle who helps them out), he has friends, and he develops feelings for little Aiko from his school. Well, things still kick off pretty fast, since Punpun’s father beats his mother so badly she ends up in hospital. I won’t go into everything that happens either, but you can see the situation moves quite quickly. And in 13 volumes, a lot happens, as you can imagine.

## Punpun and Aiko

Anyway, back to Punpun’s story, and more specifically the central relationship of the story. From the equivalent of primary school, he makes friends and meets Aiko, who is going to be one of the central points. Their relationship evolves throughout the work in a rather surprising way. At first it’s fairly basic, once again: you might think it’s just a childhood crush, but that would be too easy.

In fact, what I love about their relationship throughout *Goodnight Punpun* is how toxic it is. Aiko wants Punpun to have eyes only for her, in a way, and from the very beginning she tries to get inside his head. Punpun, for his part, since she’s his first love, lets it happen and falls into a kind of dependence on Aiko, even after they separate. When I say “separate”, they were never actually a couple, but it really is a separation in the literal sense: they take different paths and don’t see each other again for a long time.

![In a gym, a little girl in a dress pulls Punpun, a small white ghost-shaped bird, by the hand](/img/punpun-aiko.jpg "Punpun and Aiko, *Goodnight Punpun*.")

And yet this dependence is there throughout the whole period when they don’t see each other. Punpun grows up, meets other people, and even other girls he sometimes has relationships with (which go well or not; I’m thinking of one in particular, if you’ve read it, in the coffee shop…), but Aiko comes back to haunt his thoughts quite often. They eventually see each other again, but not necessarily for the better…

## Punpun’s faces

And now I have to bring up the second thing I love in this work: Punpun’s changes. Those of you who haven’t read it may not get it, but basically, I said at the start that Punpun looks like a bird. Except that’s not actually the case for the whole work. Punpun evolves, like everyone, and it shows in the way the author draws him. It can be very minor, like adding sort of spiky hair to the bird to show adolescence and the rebellious side, but it can also be much deeper…

![Seven versions of Punpun: the ghost-bird, a bird in a striped hat, a smiling triangle, a bird with drooping hair, a long beak on a shirted torso, a black head covered in eyes, and a horned black head](/img/punpun-formes.jpg "Punpun’s different forms, *Goodnight Punpun*.")

For example, the triangle form is really interesting, because it comes at a time in Punpun’s life when he shuts away his emotions: he’s neutral, he’s simple, like a triangle, the most basic shape there is to draw. And I love his two monster designs, at the moment Punpun is at the lowest point of his life. Well, I won’t go through every form of Punpun in detail either, because it could take a while, but I find it so clever to make a character evolve like that.

## To conclude

I’d like to conclude by saying that if you’ve never read this manga, it hits you like nothing else. Sure, you’ve had a few spoilers, but there’s still so much left to discover in this, to say the least, shocking work. So if you’re not afraid to venture into the life of Punpun Onodera, I can only recommend you go and buy the volumes (or read the scans, we all know, even though supporting the author matters!).
